The most common question we get from funds isn't about the scoring. It's quieter than that, and it usually arrives after the demo, in a lower voice: "Where do the decks go?"

Fair question. A pitch deck is a strange document — a founder hands their entire company to strangers, numbers and roadmap and all, in the hope of money. The organizer who collects fifty of those decks is holding fifty companies' worth of confidential material. Some of them work at places where the policy on public AI tools is one word long: no.

Today we have an answer that's better than a paragraph in our privacy policy. EvalLens is partnering with **Kaizen Finance** and **R2 Copilot**.

```text
Your decks → EvalLens methodology (6 lenses) → isolated AI lane → evidence-backed reports → your decision
```

AI prepares the evaluation. You make the final decision. That line doesn't change. What changes is where the computation can happen.

## Who we're partnering with

Two products, one team.

**R2 Copilot** builds private AI environments — the pitch, in their words, is AI that works where public AI isn't allowed. Their published materials describe encryption on the user's side, no training on user data, and infrastructure that keeps content off the public internet. R2 is a certified Microsoft Solutions Partner. We're integrating their infrastructure as an evaluation backend option for exactly the clients who ask us the question above.

**Kaizen Finance** is a launch platform that has taken **100+ projects to market** — a working ecosystem of founders, incubators, and the funds that watch them. Their portfolio companies get structured, evidence-backed evaluation with a human making the final call; our users get a bridge into a wider deal ecosystem.

## What actually changes for you

Three things, in order of how often you've asked for them.

**An isolated evaluation lane.** For organizers whose compliance teams can't sign off on public AI tools, we're wiring an option where deck analysis runs on R2's isolated infrastructure instead. Same methodology, same six lenses, same evidence-backed report — different pipes. The integration is in progress now; we're announcing it early because our users ask about it roughly weekly.

**A deeper bench of startups.** Kaizen's portfolio and incubator companies will start running through EvalLens — which means more decks, more runs, and a sharper read for everyone. Our methodology got better with every one of its 1,000+ runs so far. It will keep getting better the same way.

**Nothing else.** Your reports, your rubric, your final call — untouched. We're deliberately boring about this: a partnership should add a capability, not renovate your workflow.

## A pitch competition, this autumn

The part we're most looking forward to is the loudest one. This autumn, we're co-organizing a pitch competition with the Kaizen ecosystem — real judges, a live leaderboard, and a full evidence-backed report for every team that enters, on the EvalLens platform.

If you've read our [Nha Trang recap](/blog/evallense-goes-live-in-nha-trang), you know the format: the room reads the evidence together, argues, and decides. The machine prepares. The people rank. We'll publish details as the date firms up.

## The quiet part

Partnerships in this industry tend to be press releases about logos. We'd rather this one be judged the way we'd judge a deck: by what it makes possible, with evidence attached.

So here's the claim, stated plainly: if your decks can't touch public AI, that stops being a reason you can't use structured evaluation. And if you run a portfolio, your companies now have a shorter path to a report that tells them which slide is carrying them and which one is quietly costing them the round.
