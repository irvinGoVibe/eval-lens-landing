## Common questions

**How do I prepare a pitch deck for an AI-evaluated pitch competition?** Write the deck to be convincing without you standing next to it. The first read has no room: your slides are checked against a fixed set of criteria, the same way for every entry. So every claim needs evidence a stranger could verify, sitting next to the claim it supports, with a denominator, a timeframe, and a date on anything that can go stale.

**Will my pre-revenue startup be penalized in a structured pitch evaluation?** No. You are judged against the stage you are actually at, whether that is idea, prototype, early traction, or growth. Missing revenue at the idea stage is normal. What costs you is missing the proof your stage can produce, like customer discovery at idea stage or real usage with dates at early traction.

**What are common mistakes that lower a pitch competition score?** Five gaps hold an entry down no matter how good the rest looks: no specific user in pain, no plain mechanism for how the product relieves that pain, no named payer, no technical owner on the team, and no owner for the next milestone. A structured read does not fill gaps in your favor. If it cannot find the payer, there is no payer.

**Does an AI decide who wins the pitch competition?** No. The structured read prepares the analysis: it scores each area, shows the evidence behind it, and surfaces the questions a reviewer should ask you. A person makes the call, and the final ranking is built from human judgment. Your deck earns the shortlist, and the live round earns the win.

**How long do reviewers actually spend reading a pitch deck?** Investors spend roughly three minutes on a first pass, and only about 58% reach the last slide. So put your proof early, let each headline state its conclusion, and keep every slide to one message. A number sitting on slide nineteen was probably never seen.
