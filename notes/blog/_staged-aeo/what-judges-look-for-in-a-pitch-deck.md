## Common questions

**What do judges actually look for in a pitch deck?** Evidence a stranger could verify without you in the room. A structured evaluation scores six dimensions, problem, solution, market, business model, team, and feasibility, and ties every judgment to something on a slide. A named customer with a real number next to it beats a wall of advisor logos.

**Which parts of a pitch deck carry the most weight?** Market and team, at 0.20 each against 0.15 for the other four dimensions. That mirrors where investors already spend their time: DocSend found the team slide draws the most attention, and by 2024 that attention had grown again. A thin slide in either place costs you more than a thin slide anywhere else.

**Why does a good-looking deck score low?** Usually because ambition replaced evidence, and a rubric counts evidence. Gaps round a score toward the lower band, thin evidence can scale a whole dimension down by as much as 15 percent, and a key fact buried on slide 19 reads as missing. Reviewers spend two to four minutes on the average deck, so findable beats thorough.

**Does AI make the final decision on my pitch?** No. The AI panel scores each dimension, shows its evidence, and drafts the questions a reviewer will ask you live. Then a person sets the final score, and the ranking is built from that human score, with the AI number staying on screen only as a reference.

**How do I prepare a pitch deck for AI-assisted screening?** Three habits raise every dimension at once. Fill all ten sections the system reads, from problem through ask, because a missing section is flagged as a hole. Put the proof next to each number instead of in an appendix, and write for the least generous honest reading of every slide, since a reproducible score leaves no lucky days.
