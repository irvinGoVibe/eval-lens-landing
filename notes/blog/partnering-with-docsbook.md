Most partnership announcements describe two logos agreeing to like each other. This one started with a score.

In August, Dan Bondarev, the solo founder of [Docsbook](https://docsbook.io/), ran his pre-seed deck through EvalLens. Six judges, unanimous verdict: medium, 5.2 out of 10. The report did what reports are for: it named the one gap every judge saw independently, the deck sells one thing while the paying customer buys another. Product and founder pulled the score up. Problem and market pulled it down, because they rested on untested hypotheses.

Dan read the report and did the rare thing. He did not argue with the judges. He asked for the room.

## The trade

So here is the partnership, stated as the trade it is.

**EvalLens gives Docsbook the room.** Weekly founder-to-founder working sessions, run against the report: a scout-mode pitch run with interruptions, a truth file read out loud, one testable hypothesis per week, written protocol the same day. Not mentorship, not encouragement. The rules ban invented numbers and unmeasured claims, and every session gets scored on the same dimensions the panel used. AI prepared the analysis; Dan decides what to do with it, week by week, on the record.

**Docsbook gives EvalLens the product.** Our documentation now runs on Docsbook: repository in, branded AI-readable docs site out. We use the full plan as a demanding early user, and what we find goes straight back to Dan as the kind of feedback a founder can act on, specific, dated, and tied to a real workflow.

Both sides open their networks as results come in. Nothing in this arrangement involves either side pretending the other is further along than it is. Docsbook today is a live SaaS with working billing and one paying customer; the honest slide about that fact was the thing our judges flagged as a rare strength.

## Why we do these

Every evaluation we ship ends with the same handoff: the panel prepares the evidence, a person makes the call. A partnership like this is that handoff stretched over weeks, with a founder who chose to treat a 5.2 as a work order instead of an insult. If the format works, the numbers will show it: the session regulament sets per-dimension targets and a checkpoint at session four. We will publish what the checkpoint shows, either way.

AI prepares the analysis. The founder decides. That line is easy to print on a website. This is what it looks like as a weekly calendar invite.
